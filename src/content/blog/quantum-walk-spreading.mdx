A classical random walker on a line flips a coin and steps left or right. After $t$ steps the position distribution is binomial, and its width grows as

$$
\sigma_{\text{classical}}(t) = \sqrt{t}.
$$

The discrete-time quantum walk replaces the coin with a two-level system. Write the state as $|\psi\rangle = \sum_{x,c} \psi_{x,c}\,|x\rangle\otimes|c\rangle$ and apply one step $U = S\,(I\otimes H)$, where $H$ is the Hadamard coin

$$
H = \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}
$$

and $S$ shifts the walker left if the coin reads $|0\rangle$ and right if it reads $|1\rangle$. Because amplitudes rather than probabilities add, paths interfere: the central region cancels and the probability piles up at two fronts moving at constant speed, so that

$$
\sigma_{\text{quantum}}(t) \propto t.
$$

The computation is a few lines. For each site keep the two coin amplitudes, apply the Hadamard mixing, then shift:

```python
import numpy as np

def hadamard_walk(steps):
    N = 2 * steps + 1
    psi = np.zeros((N, 2), dtype=complex)
    psi[steps] = [1, 1j] / np.sqrt(2)          # symmetric start
    H = np.array([[1, 1], [1, -1]]) / np.sqrt(2)
    for _ in range(steps):
        psi = psi @ H.T                          # coin
        left, right = np.roll(psi[:, 0], -1), np.roll(psi[:, 1], 1)
        psi = np.stack([left, right], axis=1)    # shift
    return (np.abs(psi) ** 2).sum(axis=1)       # probability per site
```
